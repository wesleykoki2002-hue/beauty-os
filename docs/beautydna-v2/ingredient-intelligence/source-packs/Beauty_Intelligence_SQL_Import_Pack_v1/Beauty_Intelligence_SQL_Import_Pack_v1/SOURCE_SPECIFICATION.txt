Beauty Intelligence — Complete Ingredient Data Specification

Beauty Intelligence should not be built as a simple list containing “ingredient name, benefits, and ingredients to avoid.” It needs to be a versioned, evidence-backed knowledge graph and rules engine capable of answering four separate questions:

What does this ingredient do?
Under which formulation and usage conditions does it do it?
For which users and environments is it suitable or unsuitable?
How does it behave with other ingredients and complete products?

The first 150 ingredients will be the seed library, but the structure must already support thousands of ingredients, Japanese labeling names, Brazilian requirements, SkinDNA, HairDNA, weather adaptation, product matching, routine generation, warnings, explanations, and future regulatory updates.

Your existing Beauty OS Supabase remains the source of truth. Athena tracks the build, decisions, reviews, audits, and progress, but it should not hold the permanent ingredient intelligence.

1. The three intelligence layers
Layer 1 — Ingredient Intelligence

Contains canonical facts about each individual ingredient:

Identity and aliases
Cosmetic functions
Benefits and mechanisms
Effective-use conditions
Safety and tolerability
Skin, scalp, and hair suitability
Environmental relevance
Regulatory status
Evidence and confidence
Ingredient-to-ingredient interaction rules
Layer 2 — Product Intelligence

Contains information about the actual product formulation:

Full ingredient list and order
Product type and routine role
Leave-on or rinse-off
Formulation pH
Known or estimated concentration ranges
Delivery system
Texture and vehicle
Instructions and frequency
Packaging and stability
Fragrance and allergen profile
Combination effects of the complete formula
Layer 3 — User and Context Intelligence

Contains the conditions under which the recommendation is being made:

Skin profile
Hair and scalp profile
Allergies and sensitivities
Current routine
Previous reactions
Pregnancy or breastfeeding caution
Medications and dermatological treatment
Weather and season
Humidity, temperature, UV and pollution
Lifestyle and exposure
Experience with active ingredients
Preferences, budget and routine complexity

A recommendation must be generated from all three layers. An ingredient that appears appropriate by itself may not be appropriate at a particular concentration, in a particular formula, when combined with another product, or for a user with an impaired barrier.

Critical distinction

The system must distinguish:

Formulation incompatibility

The ingredients may be difficult to formulate together inside one bottle.

Routine incompatibility

Two separate finished products may create excessive irritation or instability when layered.

Tolerability burden

The ingredients may be chemically compatible but too aggressive together for a particular user.

Redundancy

The combination may be unnecessary rather than dangerous.

Synergy

The ingredients may improve complementary outcomes or tolerability.

Medical caution

The issue relates to a medical condition, prescription or sensitive population and should not be reduced to a cosmetic “mixing” rule.

The existing avoid_combining_with field should eventually become a derived customer summary, generated from approved structured interaction rules. It should not be the source of truth.

Japan’s Standards for Cosmetics contain prohibited and restricted categories and specific positive-list controls for areas such as preservatives, UV absorbers and tar colors. Ingredient records therefore need more than a generic Japan-approved flag.

Anvisa updated cosmetic-substance lists in July 2026, demonstrating why these records must be versioned and regularly rechecked rather than permanently copied into an ingredient row.

22. Rules the research agent must follow

The agent must:

Use the exact canonical INCI ingredient, not a loosely related ingredient family.
Distinguish the parent ingredient from salts, esters, derivatives, extracts and fermented forms.
Preserve Japanese labeling names separately.
Never invent concentration values.
Never invent regulatory approval.
Never infer “safe” from absence of a warning.
Never infer effectiveness from a brand claim alone.
Never create a hard compatibility rule without evidence.
State whether an interaction concerns the same formula, same routine or cumulative irritation.
Separate chemical incompatibility from user tolerability.
Record conflicting findings instead of choosing the most convenient conclusion.
Use null for unknown data rather than guessing.
Explain why a field is unknown when the missing information is important.
Attach sources to each material claim.
Mark theoretical evidence as theoretical.
Avoid universal comedogenicity conclusions.
Avoid universal Malassezia conclusions.
Avoid categorical pregnancy-safety claims.
Avoid medical diagnosis.
Default every generated record to needs_review, not approved.
Never overwrite an approved record without creating a new version.
Record the research date and applicable regulatory date.
Produce machine-readable JSON with no commentary outside the JSON when preparing import files.
Use the approved controlled vocabularies.
Flag duplicate ingredients and aliases before insertion.
23. Approval requirements

An ingredient should only move from needs_review to approved when:

Canonical identity is verified
Aliases are verified
Main functions are sourced
Main benefit claims are sourced
Safety endpoints are reviewed
Japan status is checked
Brazil status is checked
EU status is checked
Concentration claims are contextualized
Interaction claims are contextualized
Customer explanations do not overstate evidence
Conflicts and limitations are documented
A human reviewer is recorded
Review date is recorded
Source links remain accessible
The record passes automated validation

Only approved ingredient data should be used in customer-facing BeautyDNA recommendations. Missing or unresolved ingredients should continue to enter beautydna_ingredient_review_queue.

24. Build order for the first 150 ingredients

For the initial 150, the agent should generate data in this order:

Pass 1 — Identity and normalization

Canonical names, Japanese names, aliases, classifications and duplicate resolution.

Pass 2 — Functions and benefits

Formulation functions, biological actions, concerns helped and product-role fit.

Pass 3 — Formulation and concentration

pH, stability, delivery context, typical use and evidence-effective concentration ranges.

Pass 4 — Safety and suitability

Skin, scalp, hair, sensitive populations, irritation, sensitization and exposure conditions.

Pass 5 — Regulatory research

Japan, Brazil, EU and US status with dated sources.

Pass 6 — Interactions

Only evidence-backed or strongly justified interactions. Do not attempt to populate every possible ingredient pair with invented information.

Pass 7 — Customer explanations

Portuguese first for Hanna customers, with English and Japanese structured alongside it.

Pass 8 — Validation and human review

Deduplication, missing fields, conflicting evidence, source quality and approval.

This specification gives Beauty Intelligence enough depth to support product compatibility, SkinDNA, HairDNA, weather-adaptive routines, ingredient explanations, safety cautions and evidence-aware recommendations without relying on simplistic “good ingredient/bad ingredient” lists.
